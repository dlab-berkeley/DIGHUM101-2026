# Dataset Tips and Sources

There's no shortage of humanities datasets. There is, however, a dearth of well-documented datasets. When you're looking for data for DH projects, you need to assess them by the following:

- What's in the dataset?
- How well-documented is the dataset?
- How was the data collected or curated?
- What's missing from the data?

These questions can be surprisingly difficult to answer, but they are important. In DH work in particular, we value proper critiques of data and we qualify our knowledge claims by knowing our data as well as possible.

If you choose to curate your own dataset, you should also document your choices. As best as possible, try to make the above questions easy to answer for any scholars who might use your data or review your work.

If you choose to curate your own dataset, please be mindful of the time and labor this can require. Many DH projects spend the vast majority of their time in data curation and preprocessing. Those are valuable scholarly contributions, but in this class, we're prioritizing practice with Python, so please allocate most of your time working with Python and the various libraries we explore rather than data curation.
